  This document's goal is not to provide the reader with set rules, recipes or solutions for every situation but rather with some keys to initiate a learning process. We are also not here to teach how to play like Republic Commandos or ARC Troopers, so no part of our training will be included in here.

  As stated in the introduction, this document isn't here to give you step by step instructions on how to lead raids. It is good to know how to "flip" from being laid back to getting focused on the game.

  The main thing you should remember from this document is to think about your actions, reflect upon the maps, the classes, etc. Once it's become common knowledge for you, it will come naturally. ## **I) RAID PREPARATION**

### ***Announcement:***

It can be quite simple, but should mention : the host, the leader, the time, the place and eventually end with a signature.

Ping both the host and the leader so people can identify them and add your Origin/EA tags. Attendees should add you beforehand.

For the time, you can either write it down using timezones (BST/GMT or CEST/CET for Europe, EDT/EST for America and AEST for Oceania) or use a timestamp that will automatically show the proper hour to attendees depending on their timezones. This can be done using https://hammertime.cyou/fr for instance.

The place is the VC where you'll hold the raid. You can use a link so attendees can easily find it. If you intend to have less than 20 members, remember to set it up once you join the VC.

A small description could also be added to get troopers involved in your raid or to describe what kind of raid it will be. For instance "Highly tactical raid in GA, 12 attendees max". The sooner you announce your raid, the more likely you are to have attendees as people will be able to plan ahead. You can still do Flash Raids but you will have to be lucky.

The signature should mention your rank, numbers, eventually your unit as well as '104th Battalion 2nd Fleet'.

Here is an announcement format you can follow or get inspiration from :

**@Personal Computer**

- ***[Type] Raid****
- ***Host:** @[Host_discord_ID]**
- ***Lead:** @[Lead_discord_ID] *(if it's different from the host's)***
- ***Time:** [timestamp]**

**[Raid Description]**

**```**

**[Rank] CT-[XXXX]**

**[Platoon Position],[Platoon]**

**(*eventually company or 44th Special Operations Division)***

**104th Battalion 2nd Fleet**

**([Squad/Platoon/Company] Staff/Command)**

**```**

### ***Joining the VC***

Have attendees join your raid some time before it is supposed to start. It is also important that you join in advance and if you are delayed, inform them as soon as possible. You are held up to a certain standard, if you meet it or exceed it, it will help your reputation and troopers will be more inclined to listen to you.

In the beginning of this pre-raid phase, you can let your troopers chat, unwind a bit and talk to old acquaintances. You do not want to sound like a tyrant and again, if you engage in conversation with your troopers, they will get to know you better and enjoy coming to your raids. At some point however, you will need to take control of the comms so you can go through raid rules and what kind of raid this is going to be, if it had not been mentioned in the announcement.

### ***Raid Rules and Comms***

When you enunciate raid rules, try not to read them as it will be rather boring. Memorise them and synthesise them. Knowing them by heart means you will be able to apply them correctly and your troopers will listen to you more thoroughly.

Here is a quick summary of raid rules:
- Be respectful towards each other, listen to the raid leader/host and don't shout.
- Phase 2 104th for everyone, Phase 1 104th for ARFs.
- Stick to your chosen base class (Assault, Heavy, Specialist), Officer allowed for SGT+ (default p2 for SGT, Naval Officer for officers of the Starfighter Corps).
- Default weapons + DC-15/LE for Heavy.
- No heroes/villains.
- Reinforcements and/or weapons must be earned through specific qualifications, earned on your respective console. Once earned, usable on any console.
- BARC Speeder/LAAT available to everyone.
- AT-TE for Tankers only in Capital Supremacy, allowed for everyone in Galactic Assault with priority to Tankers.
- In GA, Pilots have priority for ships.
- While looking for a game, search at least 3 times to try and find a Republic game (depending on how long the search time is), then play raid rules regardless of your faction.
- If two 104th parties are facing each other, follow raid rules.

You should then cover comms and how tactical you intend them to be. "Hard Tac Comms" means no chatter, only call outs and orders, highly recommended for bigger raids or if the game is tough. This doesn't mean no one should talk, communication is key so the raid leader knows what's going on and so his troops receive orders and objectives.  "Medium Tac Comms" would be for a more laid back raid, with no major difficulties and some random chatter. "Light Tac Comms" are used for play sessions, with a few attendees and no real need for call outs and orders. You can switch between those during the raid, if you feel like the game is getting harder to win or if the comms start to degenerate for instance. Do not hesitate to tell people off or even remove them from the raid if they disturb your comms, regardless of rank. You are in control of the raid.

### ***Invites***

During this time, you should also start sending out invites to create your in game lobby. Even if someone already has a friend added and not you, send them a friend request in case they have to join back, it will also help in further raids. It is also important that you remember who is in your raid so you can give them orders but also be aware of their classes and qualifications. First so they don't break raid rules and then so you can use them accordingly in game. It can also be nice to ask them what they intend to play just before the game starts. They might adapt their playstyle to the map.

Remember to have everyone change to the appropriate ping site, even one singular attendee could drag you on other servers.

### ***Raid Type***

The last thing you should cover is what type of raid it is going to be. If it is a joint raid, will you lead the entirety of it? Will each leader handle their own unit? Do you want this to be a squad raid? If so, do you set up once you're in the game, or will you create squads beforehand? Everything that can be done before the start of the game should be covered to be as efficient as possible. This will help set the tone of your raid and in turn give you better control over it.

### ***Squad Composition***

For raid leaders that want to use a squad system with pre-made squads, it is paramount that you take into account classes and qualifications. It is then up to you to decide whether you would rather have squads made up of the same classes and qualifications or if you want to spread them out, however, here a few recommendations:

- Do not put all Aerials, Tankers or AT-RT Drivers together as they will not be able to provide spawns to their squadmates.
- Do not put only Heavies in a squad, they will be slow so it will be frustrating for them and for you. On ship, if they get wiped, you will lose a lot of firepower and it will take them a long time to come back.
- It can be worth it to have all of SF in the same squad since they can run off accomplish their own objectives. This is especially true for Republic Commandos, they should not be separated.
- Putting people from the same unit (company, platoon, squad) in the same squad can be a good idea as they are more likely to play together often.

Once your squads are made up, make sure that you designate a squad leader and use their name to call out the squad. Giving squad numbers or letters to identify them is confusing for you and your troopers whereas a quick look at their squadmates and attendees will know if you are talking to their squad. That squad leader should then add all their troopers on their friends' list if it isn't already the case. Once you get into the game, wait for the timer to start (in case you want to back out) and then ask squad leaders to leave the group so they can create their own with their squad members. If someone has difficulties receiving or sending invites, you should have them stay in the original in-game group with their squadmates and have only the other squads move out.

## **II) COMMUNICATION**

### ***Attitude***

A raid leader has to inspire confidence and exude authority. If you are too hesitant, indecisive or quiet, your troops will not follow your orders properly and might even start to doubt your leadership. This might result in a complete loss of control over the raid, with everyone talking over each other, people running off into random directions and it can be very hard to recover from that. Even if you are unsure about the orders that you give or how to proceed, do not let it transpire through your voice. If you make a wrong decision you can correct it, the important thing is that **you** make it. Your troopers will also trust you so they will follow your directives more willingly and they will not panic even if the game doesn't go as planned. This will in turn make it easier for you to lead them, boost your confidence and so on and so forth.

### ***Efficiency and concision***

When issuing orders or giving information, you should be both precise and brief. You will need to send your troops to specific command posts, have them accomplish set tasks and dispose of certain enemies. If they wander off or if your orders are not clear enough, your battle plan will not be executed properly and it could throw you off balance or result in a waste of time and resources. However, the longer it takes you to give out your commands, the more time you waste and the less likely it is for attendees to listen to them fully. Do not be polite, remove words that are unnecessary like prepositions. For instance, instead of saying "Could one of the ARC Troopers please go and take down the Maul on C? It'd be of great help.", go "Need an ARC to kill Maul Charlie". It's not much, but information flows faster, allowing time and space to give out other orders or call outs. This is especially true when doing "Hard Tac Comms", you can still say "please" and "thank you" of course, but only if you have time. This is also why you should not hesitate when you speak as it will clutter comms. Formulate your sentence beforehand, be sure that it is what you want to say and then communicate. Anticipate, try and think about your next moves before you actually have to make them.

### ***Vocabulary***

We will not provide a long list of words that can be used in specific situations, but here are some basic ones. You can find them, and more, in military talk or in aeronautical conversations for instance. Remember, everyone has to understand you so refrain from using obscure terms.

It is highly recommended to use the aeronautical alphabet, also called NATO phonetic alphabet, when saying letters, for instance with command posts. You could also use the *Aurebesh* names but people are less familiar with them. They are used by the in-game advisor though.

A - Alpha/*Auresh*
B - Bravo/*Besh*
C - Charile/*Cresh*
D - Delta/*Dorn*
E - Echo/*Esk*

Some words allow you to acknowledge orders or information faster, for instance:

Copy, Roger, Understood, Wilco (will comply), Will do, Affirm(ative)

If you can't do something, you could use:

Negative, Unable, Not doable

Use simple words when giving out orders:

Move, Capture/Take/Attack, Hold/Defend, Kill/Take down

It's also important to have short call outs for enemies, mainly using their classes or reinforcement name and then stuff like Aerial/Jet/Rocket, B2/Super, (Droi)deka, BX/Commando, AAT/Tank or ARC, RC/Commando and TX/Tank. Using AAT and TX allows you to make the difference between a friend or a foe, same goes for RC and BX.

Some abbreviations like LZ for Landing Zone, ETA for Estimated Time of Arrival or OBJ for objective can also be useful but are more situational. It also means that your troopers have to know what they mean, it's taught in some units but not all. For more vocabulary, do not hesitate to look through some military or aeronautical talks. The most important thing is that you keep it short and understandable. Remember that we have a lot of different nationalities and accents.

Sometimes, if you want to make sure you understood something correctly or confirm an order you've received or given, you can also "read it back". So for instance as a raid leader, if you say "X squad move C and hold" X squad's leader can say "Moving and holding C, X squad" or "X squad moving C and holding".

### ***Troop movement***

Knowing how to traverse the maps is essential, it will allow you to send some of your party on flanks, block off some areas or get to a certain point faster. You should also think about where your troops are currently located. Let's say you've sent your two Aerials on D, if you call them back to A, it will take them a lot longer than if you had a squad between A and C moving there. Likewise, if you don't have a full 20 man raid, keep in mind where the randoms will go. C is a very central point, so there will be a lot of players around it. If your homepoints are captured, your team will most likely attempt to take them back.

This is also true for enemies. If you just took D, expect them to come back to take it. Or if they took C, expect them to go for your homepoints. You should either move to counter them, making sure you defend the proper points, or send troopers where you know they won't be so you can quickly grab a command post or enter the reactor room. These troop movements can only be learned by playing the game and trying some yourselves.

Your squads do not have to be in constant movement. Sometimes, raid leaders will move on to the next point as soon as they have captured their current objectives. First, clear out any contesting forces. Then, think about whether you need to push out or if it's best to stay and hold your positions.

When you are losing a command post, before sending all of your troopers back to contest it, make sure that you leaving your position will not cost you even more and figure out if it's not maybe better to push another area. A typical example would be on Naboo, your team holds A, B and C. You are losing C. Is E well protected? If not you could have some of your troopers go capture it while the rest pulls back in defence of B.

This still applies to the ship phase. Going through the central corridor is often a death sentence on the droid ship, however, moving directly to the spawn side after taking the doors can surprise the enemy and allow you to pierce through their defences.

Some minor information that can be useful if you cannot take down the enemy's capital ship in 1 phase or if you manage to defend yours. Once you get back to the ground, you will have some command posts already. They are as follows:

Kamino : E, D to Republic / B, C to CIS
Geonosis : A, B to Republic / C, D to CIS
Kashyyyk : A, B to Republic / D, E to CIS
Felucia : E, D to Republic / A, B to CIS
Naboo : C, E to Republic / A, B to CIS

## **IV) CLASS AND QUALIFICATION USAGE**

In addition to allowing you to make better use of your troops, knowing what each class and reinforcement does will help you counter them. Asking a squad to run straight towards an overloading B2 is a death sentence.

When it comes to SF, or even if you have other more experienced staff members in your raid, if you are unsure as to what to do in order to win the game, you can always ask for advice. They might also provide you with some. It can be hard to take in, especially if it's a hard game and you're doing everything you can but it might unlock the situation. There is no shame in it.

### ***Assault and Rifleman/Airborne***

Fast moving troopers, they are the ones who will be the first on points and take the brunt of the initial assault. They are also the most common class in the 104th, so you will always have some at your disposal. If you have a rifleman or Airborne, you can expect them to be above average troopers and if they use CR2 they will be very good at close quarter combat. However, Assaults tend to just run from one point to the next, so make sure to direct them to the proper points and to tell them to hold if needed.

### ***Heavy and Anti-Armour***

Heavies are slower but pack a lot of firepower and have more health. They have access to grenades and sentry, which makes them good at dealing a lot of damage to a lot of targets simultaneously at a short to medium range. They are good at defending points and can help a lot with taking down Heroes/Villains and Tanks, but with support or they will be picked off. On ship, they will be essential to take down the reactors, so try and protect them. A lot of people just tell them to focus the reactors and leave them there, but if they do not have a bit of protection, they will become the main priority for enemy forces and will be killed before being able to deal any substantial damage. When you move them around the battlefield, remember that it takes longer for them. Anti-Armour troopers are specifically trained to take Tanks down and thanks to the T-21 can also act as snipers.

### ***Officer***

Officers are the main support unit in the game. Since they are also all ranked sergeant or above, it would be wise to spread them out across your squads so they can act as squad leaders and boost their troopers. They can be particularly effective on ship with the defuser that disables any explosive device in a disk around them or the weapon disruptor that overheats all enemy weapons caught in its area of effect. Squad shields can be rather effective when you want to protect a certain objective, may it be a reactor or a command post. If an enemy penetrates that shield, it should be taken down as soon as possible so you can deal with them, it protects them as much as it protects you.

### ***Specialist and Marksman/Scout Trooper***

Specialists are the weaker classes in terms of health. Like Assaults, they can move fast from point to point thanks to infiltration that will also reveal nearby enemies. It provides them with a short range weapon but Specialists are of course more efficient at long range. Try to place them on higher ground, with a long line of sights. The shock grenade is very useful at controlling areas, especially on ship. It can also deal damage through shields and blocks, even if it's not a lot it will prevent the enemy hero or Droideka from regaining health. With access to the NT, Marksmen are particularly good with long distance fights, so use them accordingly. Using the Ion shot deals a lot of damage to the reactors, so if you can't quite reach them you could ask your Marksmen to switch.

### ***TX-130 Driver***

You can only have 2 TX-130s at a time, and on Felucia they are also allowed to use the AT-TE. Tanks are very effective at dealing damage to large groups of enemies, other tanks or heroes. However, they are a big target. If the enemy starts using Ion weapons, try and take them down quickly so your tankers don't get instantly picked off. Their size also means that some areas will be unreachable for them. A tank can hold a point on its own, or support a push for instance, but they should also be spread out. Having two at the same spot is overkill and you're wasting resources. Keep in mind that you cannot spawn on them.

### ***ARF Trooper***

ARF Troopers are very versatile, since they can use all base classes. They're also skilled players so they can be used to capture objectives in smaller numbers. Consider asking them to switch classes according to your needs, or ask them what they intend to play and then use them accordingly. For example, if you don't have any heavies and you have to destroy the reactors, ask them to use that class to deal damage.

### ***AT-RT Driver***

AT-RTs are fast moving units that can help reveal enemies and destroy enemy tanks. They deal quite a lot of damage but the driver is exposed. They should be used to quickly move from one point to the other and support pushes. Keep in mind that you cannot spawn on them.

### ***Jet Trooper***

Aerials are the fastest moving units of the game, apart from perhaps droidekas. They can also reach high places. It is a powerful unit that deals a lot of damage, especially with its rocket launcher. It also costs less battle points than other reinforcements. Some Aerials have a tendency to spawn it, take their rocket out, jump and shoot then die and repeat. Like Assaults, they also often go from one point to the other if you don't tell them what to do. They are very good at countering heroes because they can evade them, and if well coordinated can get behind tanks to deal a lot of damage. It is a rather hard qualification to pass so they are often skilled players, even if they run base classes. As a last resort, you could use them so suicide bomb reactors but they only have so many battle points.

### ***ARC Trooper***

ARCs are among the most skilled players of the unit, however they aren't invincible. You can use them to take down enemy heroes or reinforcements, clear points on their own but if they get overwhelmed they won't be able to do much so it's good to still have support ready if needed. Don't be afraid to ask them to accomplish tasks, they can disobey if they believe they should be doing something else that will be more useful to the team, but generally speaking they'll do what you require. You can also leave them unsupervised, calling them in when you really need them. They can deal a lot of damage at a long range using the Power blast, to counter enemy snipers for instance. Their class is tailored for killing stuff, rather than dealing a lot of damage to objectives or acting as a shield for regular troopers. If you're on the droid side, BXs move fast and are very good at both medium and close range. They can also jump higher than most units. They'll be efficient at taking down heroes or other reinforcements.

### ***Republic Commando***

RCs will do their best to ensure that the game is won. Like ARCs, you can ask them to accomplish certain objectives and they'll do it if they think it will help the team. An RC squad can also run things on its own, its members have been trained extensively to handle most situations or adapt to new ones. Contrarily to ARCs though, they run as a squad, so keep them together. Sometimes, they might head into another VC so they can do their comms to not clutter the main raid's comms and focus on their own tasks. RCs can deal a lot of damage to objectives thanks to the Anti-Armour attachment as well as dispatch Tanks very quickly. Like Heavies though, if they're not slightly protected on ship while they focus on the reactor, they can be quickly taken down. On the CIS side, they gain access to both B2 and Droideka. The first packs a lot of damage but is slow moving, the second is very useful to rush points and move through enemy lines to provide spawns later on. They are a bit weaker though.

## **V) SQUAD LEADING**

### ***Communication***

Leading a squad and leading a raid are two different things. When you lead an entire raid, you have to manage a rather large group of players, or several squads and keep in mind where everyone is, elaborate a strategy to capture different points, etc. As a squad leader, you only have to manage up to 3 other troopers and you focus on one objective at a time. It doesn't mean you can stay idle and wait for orders to come in.

First off, when you receive an order, you have to acknowledge it or inform the raid leader if you can't, he's counting on you. Then you should make sure that it's been relayed to your troopers, eventually by reading it back, i.e repeating the order.

Once you start moving, ensure your squadmates are actually following the given directive. If they aren't, remind them and if it becomes an issue report it to the raid leader so they can take the appropriate measures.

When you've accomplished your task, either ask your raid leader for further orders if they haven't already or inform them of your next move. You can take initiative, and are even encouraged to do so, but always communicate about it. Your raid leader should know where his troops are at all times. Likewise, if you can't accomplish your objective, inform the raid leader and eventually explain why.

A good squad leader will make it a lot easier for a raid leader, it's also why they should be chosen with care.

### ***Movement***

When moving your squad, try to do so as a group but still spread out so you don't all die because of one explosive. Being the leader, it's also your responsibility to provide spawns to your squadmates. You can split off for pincer manoeuvres for instance, but otherwise try and stay grouped up. You can also use your trooper's qualifications and classes, for instance having the aerial fly at the back of the tank while the assault runs in front, boosted by an officer and a heavy moving with his sentry ready to hit the back of the tank.

## **VI) AFTER ACTION**

### ***Ending the raid***

Once you're done with your raid, unless you absolutely have to go, spend some time with your attendees. You can play another game of blast, ewok hunt or even GA or CS without raid rules to unwind. Don't hesitate to ask for feedback, even if you're a CDR, there's always going to be something you can improve on. This will also help with your reputation again, it's not the most important thing, but if people like you and enjoy your raids they're likely to attend again. They'll also emulate you and will start a virtuous circle.

### ***Attendance***

Before anyone leaves, take a screenshot of the VC so you know who attended. You can use that in the AAR (see below). Then a representative from each company will log their company's members. As the raid host or lead, you will have to log staff member attendance in the Resilient. If someone from SFC is present, they'll have to do it in the Triumphant.

### ***After Action Report (AAR)***

Your report doesn't have to be super extensive. It should mention the list of attendees, the number of games played on what map, side and its outcome. With a short description of what happened. Then if anything out of the ordinary happened, may it be that a trooper or squad leader performed really well, or that an attendee was being disruptive. If you do that, also communicate it to the concerned member's Chain of Command. The AAR is mainly for you to remember what went right, what went wrong and how to do better next time.

You can also designate an MVP, it doesn't have to be the attendee who scored the highest on the leaderboard, but rather the one who helped your team the most. Below is an example of a template:

**[Rank] [Name] CT-[XXXX]**

**Locations deployed:**

**[Game mode] | [Map] | [Faction] | [Outcome]**

**Units:**

**[List of attendees sorted by unit]**

**Session summary:**

**[Description of what happened]**

**MVP:**

**Positives/Negatives: [Self criticism and feedback]**
