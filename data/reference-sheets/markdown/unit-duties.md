## Raid/Event Leading

### Primary Duty

### Fostering Unit Cohesion Through Raids and Events
The foremost duty of every staff member is to lead raids and organize events that strengthen the bonds within the unit or subunit. These activities can range from standard Star Wars Battlefront 2 raids to more casual events like movie nights. Regardless of whether the event occurs at the fireteam level, company level, or any tier in between, the core responsibility of a staff member is to ensure the community remains active, engaged, and united.

### The Raid Process

### Request, Execution, and Logging
Organizing a raid or event involves a structured process that includes requesting, executing, and logging the activity:

#### Requesting a Raid
Staff members must be familiar with the process of requesting raids. The procedure may vary depending on the level of the raid:

1. Platoon-Level Raids: Some platoons handle their raid requests internally within their platoon.
2. Company-Level Raids: For events that are planned at the company level or higher, raid requests should be submitted through the designated channels, such as the "Raid Request" channel in the Resilient server.

#### Executing the Raid
When leading a raid, staff members must ensure they are well-versed in the rules and regulations governing the event. This includes:

1. Adhering to Established Protocols: All raids and events must comply with the unit's guidelines to avoid any infractions that could result in retraining by the inspection team.
2. Maintaining Order and Discipline: Effective raid execution requires maintaining clear communication, ensuring all participants understand their roles, and adapting to changing circumstances in real-time.

#### Logging the Activity
Proper documentation of raids and events is essential:

1. After-Action Reports (AARs): The format and necessity of logging vary by event. For major raids, AARs should be submitted in the designated channel (e.g., AAR channel in the 104th). For company and platoon-level raids, the logging procedure will follow the rules set by the respective unit.
2. Mandatory Logging: Regardless of the format, all activities must be logged within the platoon and the Resilient server for staff reference. This step is crucial to meet the activity requirements set by the platoon for Clone Troopers (CTs) and by the 104th for their staff.

### Importance of Logging and Documentation

Accurate and timely logging is perhaps the most critical task for staff members. It ensures compliance with activity rules and standards, helps monitor engagement levels, and provides a record of participation and performance. This is especially important given the activity requirements that both the platoon and the 104th place on their members.

## Training

### Training Introduction

Training new cadets is a critical responsibility for maintaining a cohesive and effective unit. Many platoons have established dedicated training departments to ensure that new members are adequately prepared to integrate with other Clone Troopers (CTs) and understand the inner workings of the 104th. Effective training goes beyond basic gameplay knowledge; it fosters a sense of belonging and camaraderie, helping cadets quickly adapt to the unit's culture and expectations.

### Core Elements of Comprehensive Training Program

### Game Mechanics and Class Tips
New cadets should receive thorough training on the game's mechanics, including controls, movement, and combat strategies. It's important to provide specific tips and tricks tailored to their assigned class, such as effective weapon use, defensive maneuvers, and teamwork strategies that enhance their contribution to the unit during raids.

### Introduction to Raid Protocols
Cadets should be taught how raids are typically conducted, including:

1. Roles and Responsibilities: Understanding their role within the raid and how it fits into the overall strategy.
2. Communication Standards: Proper use of voice channels to ensure clear and effective communication.
3. Common Tactics and Procedures: Familiarity with standard operating procedures.

### Orientation to Unit Structure and Operations
Training should include a comprehensive overview of how the platoon and company are structured, including:

1. Hierarchy and Chain of Command: Understanding the roles and responsibilities of different ranks and positions within the platoon and company.
2. Unit Functions and Coordination: Insight into how various elements within the 104th collaborate during operations, as well as inter-unit coordination.

### Navigating Key Platforms
A critical part of training is guiding cadets through the use of essential platforms and tools:

1. KMC, Resilient, and 104th Platforms: Teaching cadets how to use KMC, Resilient. This includes guiding them through checkpoints, explaining processes, and engaging with the community.

### Welcoming and Integrating Cadets into the Community
A well-rounded training program should also emphasize creating a welcoming environment for cadets:

1. Fostering a Positive Experience: Trainers should actively encourage cadets to engage with their peers, participate in community events, and feel comfortable asking questions or seeking guidance.
2. Mentorship and Support: Assigning experienced CTs or staff members as mentors can help cadets acclimate more quickly, providing them with a point of contact for any concerns or uncertainties.

### Continuous Learning and Adaptation

While certain aspects of gameplay may be left for cadets to learn through experience, a good training program should establish a foundation that encourages ongoing development. By equipping cadets with both the technical skills and knowledge of unit culture, we set them up for success and ensure they have a fulfilling experience within the 104th.

## Logistics

### Logistics Introduction

The logistics department plays a crucial role in ensuring the smooth functioning of the unit by managing a wide range of tasks that support operational readiness and efficiency. Responsibilities within the logistics department can vary significantly, with some duties delegated to the platoon command while others are managed directly by the logistics team. This flexibility allows each unit to tailor its logistics operations based on its specific needs and organizational structure.

### Activity Checks

Activity checks are a method used by some platoons to ensure that all members remain engaged and responsive within the unit. While not universally implemented across all platoons, those that do use activity checks may refer to them by different names. The core purpose remains the same: to prompt all members to respond to a specific message or signal, confirming their active status.

These checks often come with disciplinary consequences for members who fail to respond, except those who have been officially placed on Leave of Absence (LOA). The Logistics Department is typically responsible for conducting these checks and handling the follow-up process:

### Execution of Activity Checks
The Logistics Department issues the activity check, monitors responses from all members, and verifies who has responded within the given timeframe.

### Managing Leave of Absence (LOA) Exceptions
Members on an approved LOA are excused from responding to activity checks. The Logistics Department cross-references responses with the list of members on LOA to ensure that those who are absent for legitimate reasons are accounted for.

### Reporting to the Disciplinary Department
Any members who do not respond to the activity check and are not on an LOA are reported to the Disciplinary Department for further action, which may include issuing strikes or other penalties as per the unit's regulations.

### Documentation and Logging
The process for logging activity checks varies between units:

- Company-Wide Logging: Some units log activity check results at the company level.
- Platoon-Specific Channels: Others may use specific channels within their platoon for logging responses.
- Official Roster Sheets: Additionally, some units maintain official roster sheets to record activity check outcomes, ensuring transparency and accountability.

By maintaining regular activity checks, the Logistics Department helps ensure that all members are active and engaged, contributing to the overall effectiveness and cohesion of the unit.

### Medic Bay: NFFC/LOA

The Medic Bay serves as the designated area where Clone Troopers (CTs) and staff members officially indicate their status as Not-Fit-For-Combat (NFFC) or on Leave of Absence (LOA). The specific rules regarding the duration of NFFC or LOA statuses may differ across units, including how long someone can be on these statuses without facing potential consequences.

## Responsibilities of the Logistics Department
The Logistics Department is primarily responsible for managing and maintaining records related to NFFC and LOA, including:

### Logging and Documentation
The Logistics Department logs all NFFC and LOA requests, ensures that appropriate roles are assigned, and, in some cases, updates nicknames on the Discord server to reflect a member's absence. This helps to clearly communicate the individual's status to the rest of the unit.

### Tracking and Monitoring
The duration of each LOA or NFFC status is tracked meticulously, usually within the unit's official roster sheet. This tracking ensures that all members are accounted for and that their expected return dates are clearly documented.

### Communication and Follow-Up
The Logistics Team also takes on the responsibility of contacting members who have exceeded their designated LOA or NFFC duration or who fail to report back on their scheduled return date. This follow-up process ensures that any extended absences are addressed and that the unit maintains operational readiness.

By efficiently managing the Medic Bay, the Logistics Department helps maintain clear communication regarding member availability and readiness, contributing to the unit's overall effectiveness and cohesion.

### Activity Report

The Logistics Department is responsible for monitoring and recording the activity levels of Clone Troopers (CTs) and staff members by analyzing raid logs from each platoon. This process helps determine how active each member has been over a specified period.

Activity tracking is typically documented either in the unit's official roster sheet or through designated channels within the unit's communication platforms. Accurate logging ensures that all participation is accounted for and provides a transparent record of each member's engagement.

These reports serve a dual purpose:

### Assessing Member Engagement
By tracking activity, the Logistics Department can identify members who have not met the required activity thresholds.

### Referring to the Disciplinary Department
Members who fail to meet the minimum activity requirements may be referred to the Disciplinary Department for further action, such as receiving a strike or other penalties, in accordance with the unit's regulations.

By maintaining comprehensive activity reports, the Logistics Department plays a crucial role in upholding the unit's standards of participation and readiness.

### Roster Updates

Roster updates are managed by either the **Platoon NCO** or the **Logistics Department**. These updates include a variety of personnel changes and administrative tasks, such as:

- Adding New Members: Recording new recruits who have joined the unit.
- Tracking Departures: Documenting members who leave the unit.
- Completion of Training: Updating the roster to reflect members who have completed their training.
- Qualifications and Class Changes: Recording new qualifications, certifications, or changes in member roles or classes.

All changes are meticulously documented in a comprehensive roster file, ensuring an accurate and up-to-date record of the unit's personnel and their statuses.

## Disciplinary

### Disciplinary Department

The **Disciplinary Department** is responsible for enforcing the unit's rules and maintaining order by issuing strikes and, when necessary, placing members on suspension. While the Logistics Department identifies which members are subject to disciplinary actions, the Disciplinary Department serves as the executing authority.

## Key Responsibilities

### 1. Issuing and Tracking Strikes
The Disciplinary Department issues strikes to members who fail to meet activity requirements or violate unit regulations. These strikes are carefully tracked, as some may be removed after a designated period of good behavior or compliance.

### 2. Removing Members
The department is also responsible for removing members who accumulate too many strikes, in accordance with unit policy.

### 3. Managing Suspensions
In certain cases, the department may place a member on suspension. This action is typically taken for:
- Behavioral Issues: When a member demonstrates unacceptable behavior within the unit, they may be temporarily suspended from chat and other activities while the situation is addressed.
- Server Suspensions: If a member is suspended on the main server, they may also be suspended within the unit.

By executing these disciplinary actions, the Disciplinary Department helps uphold the unit's standards of conduct and ensure a positive and respectful environment for all members.

## Meetings

### Regular Meetings

Regular meetings at all levels of the unit are essential for maintaining effective communication and addressing potential issues proactively. Meetings should be held consistently across various levels—such as Fireteam (FT), Squad, Staff, Platoon, and Company—to ensure information flows smoothly throughout the unit.

## Purpose of Regular Meetings

### Foster Communication
Regular meetings promote open dialogue and allow staff to raise concerns, share updates, and collaborate on solutions.

### Early Issue Resolution
By discussing matters regularly, staff can identify and address issues at the earliest stage, often before they escalate to higher levels.

### Alignment and Coordination
Meetings help ensure that everyone, from the lowest level up to company leadership, is aligned on objectives, strategies, and operational plans.

By establishing a routine for these meetings, the unit ensures a culture of communication and transparency, enabling more efficient and cohesive operations.

## Reports

### Reporting Structure

Reports are generated at various levels within the unit, ultimately contributing to a comprehensive Company Report that is submitted to the Resilient for Command to review and assess.

## Reporting Structure

### Bottom-Up Reporting
The reporting process typically begins at the Fireteam level, where Squad Commanders request reports from Fireteam Leaders (FTLs) regarding the status and performance of their teams.

### Consolidation at Higher Levels
This information is then compiled into Squad Reports, which are passed up to Platoon Command. Platoon Command integrates these reports into a consolidated Platoon Report.

### Company-Level Assessment
Finally, Company Command gathers all Platoon Reports, including insights from the Squad and Fireteam levels, to create a comprehensive Company Report.

## Content of Reports
These reports should transparently cover key areas such as activity levels, suggestions for improvement, and any complaints or concerns. Clear and honest reporting ensures that all levels of the unit are aligned and that any issues or recommendations are communicated effectively up the chain of command.

## Leaving Logs

### Maintaining Accurate Records

When a member leaves the unit, it is essential to maintain accurate records at multiple levels. While leaving logs are sometimes maintained at the platoon level to allow the Logistics Department to update the roster, it is equally important to log these departures in the Resilient. This log should include the reason for the departure, whether the member left voluntarily or was removed, to ensure transparency and proper documentation.

## Census

### Regular Census

Regular censuses are conducted to provide an overview of the different units within the Resilient. Typically managed by the Platoon NCO, the census reports detail the number of members in command positions, within each squad, and in reserves, effectively summarizing the unit's active personnel.

The purpose of these censuses is to offer Command a quick snapshot of the unit's current strength and distribution, helping to determine if adjustments are needed—such as reallocating cadets to specific units based on their staffing needs.
